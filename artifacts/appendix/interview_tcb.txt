[AX Consulting Interview Transcript — TCB (Technology Credit Bureau) Evaluation Team / 2026.06]

Q. How does an evaluation begin?
A. An evaluation request is filed electronically through a bank terminal or the integrated platform. Once received, the TCB business team checks the applicant company's basic information and emails an upload link on the integrated platform, requesting scraping consent and document submission. If the company consents to financial/non-financial data scraping, the financial statements, business registration certificate, and shareholder status are collected automatically, while the company profile and product catalog are uploaded manually. A technology summary sheet can also be filled in through the link, but it is not mandatory, so in most cases it is collected on site during the due-diligence visit.

Q. What about the due-diligence visit?
A. ECN distinguishes new versus re-evaluation cases: for a new case it assigns an on-site inspector based on region and workload, and for a re-evaluation it assigns a phone inspector. The inspector visits (or calls), collects any missing documents, and writes and uploads the inspection report to the inspection system. After that, an evaluator is assigned at random, and as a rule the evaluation is done on the same day according to the bank's designated schedule.

Q. What does the evaluation process itself look like?
A. The evaluator checks the application information, the inspection progress, and the issuance history, and reviews whether the documents are complete. If anything is missing, a supplementary request is issued. After a conflict-of-interest consent, the investigation starts. The company profile is entered first by the evaluation support team and then confirmed by the evaluation team — the company overview based on the business registration certificate, the standard industry classification search and selection, company history, representative information including education and career, credit-bureau status, and shareholder information. These are pulled from the corporate registry or the shareholder register, or written in manually.

Q. What about the company-overview section?
A. We check the previous technology grade, and after assessing the quality of the inspection materials we request a re-inspection if they are inadequate. Company information is carried over from the previous evaluation; we confirm the technology-evaluation target industry and the credit-exposure industry, search and select the industrial-technology classification, and choose new-growth items, climate-technology classification, and the evaluation basis (Article 16, Paragraph 1 or 2). Financial information cannot use scraping because of parsing-performance issues, so it is updated manually. We write up the main business areas, products, revenue share, and technologies, and for patents we pull representative patents from KIPRIS via real-time scraping, select them, and read the published full text to confirm the "problem to be solved" and the "effect of the invention" and enter the supporting basis. A draft technology-evaluation opinion can also be generated with GPT.

Q. What are the on-site technology inspection items?
A. There are eight items — management capability of the owner, management ability, technology-development capability, productization capability, profit outlook, technological innovativeness, market status, and product superiority. For each item, checking the boxes produces a rule-based score, and some opinions are auto-generated via guidance, but in the end the evaluator judges, selects, edits, compares against the previous grade, and saves. Research-and-development organization and certifications can be scraped via API (Inno-Biz, venture-firm, national R&D), and for market status the TDB report is pulled in so the market size is evaluated automatically. The basis for technological innovativeness uses GPT summary generation.

Q. What is the final stage?
A. In the technology-analysis stage the evaluation opinions are written — the technology opinion is GPT-generated then reviewed and edited, and the market opinion is often copy-pasted from an existing report or TDB and then edited. The summary and detailed opinions for the eight items are confirmed and written, and in the comprehensive evaluation the grade is confirmed and the comprehensive opinion is GPT auto-generated and written up. Once the report is generated and a review is requested, a reviewer is assigned at random; the system automatically performs error and rule-based checks while the reviewer checks the qualitative items. After the evaluation team leader's final approval, the report is dispatched.

Q. Where does the most time go?
A. The due-diligence visit (including travel) and writing the evaluation opinions. Even with a GPT draft, the opinions involve a lot of repeated copy-paste and editing, and research-type tasks such as market-data searches and KIPRIS checks are also large. The manual entry of financial information is another regrettable time sink.
